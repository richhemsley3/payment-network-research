How a partner gets in

A bank applies for a licence through Visa Access after a regional team confirms eligibility, and the review runs on four axes: regulatory permissions, financial and compliance standing, and the rest (Visa · Visa). Everyone who is not a bank is an agent. A member must register every third-party agent through Program Request Management, per acquirer, after a senior officer's review and an on-site inspection (Visa · Visa · Visa · Visa). The published agent fee schedule dates from 2016, 5,000 dollars initial and annual for ISOs and payment facilitators, and the only published processing time is from the same FAQ, five to seven business days (Visa · Visa). Visa Partner now offers a self-service registration tool with three paths, ending in a business ID and a public directory listing (Visa Inc.). Fintechs take Fast Track, which is eligibility criteria and enablement partners, with cost benefits described and no prices (Visa · Visa Inc. (Visa Partner)). Technology vendors take Visa Ready, and one track publishes both a timeline and a cost (Visa · Visa). ATM deployers are ISO-ATM agents, and an ATM must be owned or sponsored by a Visa or Plus client to qualify (Visa).

Two shapes of front door

The properties sort into platforms and gateways. A platform puts the catalogue, the documentation and the sign-up on the public side and keeps only credentials behind the account: Visa, Mastercard, American Express, AP+ Developers, Dev.Elo and the UPI Open Platform work this way. A gateway shows a welcome line and asks the reader to identify themselves before anything else: the Discover Developer Center, JCB Partner Online, EASI, the PULSE client website. The Interac Innovation Hub is a platform by shape and a gateway by rule, since access to specific products needs an existing relationship with an issuer (This research, observed in the browser). Neither shape is wrong for a network that onboards through a sponsor and a contract. The difference is what a stranger can judge before asking to be let in.

What the account buys, and what the contract buys

Every platform draws the same line. The account buys a project, sandbox keys and mock data. The contract buys certification and production. Visa issues certification and production credentials only once a signed agreement is in place (Visa), and says onboarding can take two weeks or several months (Visa). Method

Desk research, walkthroughs and sentiment gathering ran between September 25 and September 27, 2026, against public sources only. Nothing behind a login was used for any network, including Discover, so the comparison is symmetric. No account was created, no credentials were entered and no form was submitted on any site. Cookie banners were declined where a decline existed and left unanswered where they were accept-only.

Every network was read against the same twelve dimensions: identity and reach, services by partner type, partner-facing surfaces, how a partner joins and certifies, documentation and sandbox, public pricing, operating rules, service and support, news since September 2024, sentiment by audience, the observed walk and recorded demonstrations. Each claim is a row in a ledger before it is a sentence, with the page it was read on, a verbatim quote of twenty-five words or fewer, the publisher, the date published where the page states one, the date read, and a label. Verified means read on a primary document: the network's own documentation, rules, fee schedules or filings, a regulator, a court, or the browser on the day. Reported means trade press, analyst work, practitioner writing, a forum post or a third-party demonstration. Vendor means the network's or a vendor's claim about itself with no outside check.

Walkthroughs ran logged out in a headless browser at 1440 by 1000 pixels through a fixed station script (landing, what is visible without an account, catalogue, documentation depth, the sandbox or sign-up path to its first wall, certification, support, status and change, search, an assistive check, the landing at 390 pixels, and the first useful artefact a stranger can obtain), and every property with a public partner surface was then read by hand in the browser pane. The rungs used to compare portals describe choices, not scores, and only two counts are reported as facts: clicks to documentation and clicks to the first wall.

Sentiment was gathered with one fixed query set per network (developer, API, sandbox, certification, onboarding, support, fees for acquirers, processors, merchants and ATM operators, plus two network-specific terms) over January 2023 to September 2026. Where a query returned thirty items or fewer, all were read. Otherwise the fifteen most relevant and the fifteen most recent were read and the report says so. Each item was coded by audience, stance and topic, and each audience is reported with its tier: Adequate at ten or more items from three or more sources, Thin at three to nine, Absent at two or fewer. Recorded demonstrations were found by the same query discipline across official channels, conference and webinar archives, podcasts and app stores. Only captions were pulled from video, never the file, and frames were captured from the public player at the timestamp cited.

Every figure, ownership statement, date, fee and absence claim was then given to three independent readers who were asked to refute it from public sources without seeing the original citation. A claim survives if two of the three could not refute it. Claims that failed, or whose page had changed with no archived copy, are listed in the section on what this research could not establish and are not carried as findings.

Every network gates participation behind a signed agreement, and only Visa publishes the application steps for a direct licence. Mastercard exposes its application form, and American Express and Discover route prospects to a representative or contact form {{rcite:visa-004+mastercard-082+amex-035+dgn-035+dgn-036+amex-466}}. Run: money

On every network studied, money runs on daily files and reports that the partner must reconcile itself. Visa issues VSS reports, Mastercard six GCMS cycles and Amex one Daily Activity Report {{rcite:visa-649+visa-625+mastercard-525+amex-522}}. Settlement and billing are separate streams. Mastercard bills weekly through MCBS, Visa invoices through its Online Invoice Portal, and both collect fees by ACH in the US {{rcite:mastercard-553+mastercard-546+visa-654+visa-631}}. The UK regulator found issuers generally cope with Mastercard and Visa fee information. Acquirers do not, so the pain concentrates on the acquiring side {{rcite:cross-407+cross-191+mastercard-536}}. The weakest link is tying an invoice line to the transactions that triggered it. Portal bulletin codes do not match invoice codes, and some Visa bulletins omitted the billing event {{rcite:cross-340+visa-628+mastercard-538}}. Visa and Mastercard each told the UK regulator they could not supply that fee logic within a year. Mastercard cited many billing events and poorly documented legacy systems {{rcite:cross-229+mastercard-563+visa-667}}. Regulators are now setting a floor. UK rules require a unique billing identifier and fee logic from July 2027, and the RBA expects machine-readable invoices at no extra cost {{rcite:cross-379+cross-376+cross-064+cross-342}}. Interchange downgrades are found after the fact, in processor reports. Mastercard's parameter file lists rate designators but omits the rates, so acquirers cannot compute expected interchange from it {{rcite:visa-642+visa-643+mastercard-516+mastercard-518}}. American Express is the clearest public counterexample. Its GNS manual publishes settlement timing, fee amounts and two-way late compensation, and its merchant files state the net payment arithmetic {{rcite:amex-529+amex-513+amex-531+amex-533}}. Processors and programme managers fill the gap. Stripe and Lithic turn raw network files into settlement records with states, and vendors sell invoice analysis to acquirers and issuers {{rcite:visa-639+visa-616+cross-388+cross-355}}. The Discover family is split. PULSE sells a one-page invoice and single-point gateway settlement, while Discover keeps US acquirer rates confidential and misclassified cards for sixteen years without telling merchants {{rcite:pulse-012+pulse-125+dgn-063+dgn-462+dgn-244}}. The public record is thin on current settlement manuals, invoice layouts and analytics screens for all four networks. Most detail comes from processor republications and regulator files {{rcite:visa-621+mastercard-550+amex-555+dgn-063}}.

Run: disputes and fraud

Every network runs the same skeleton: a fraud report or claim, a chargeback, a response, pre-arbitration, then a ruling. Each wraps it in its own tool, vocabulary and clock {{rcite:visa-313+mastercard-211+amex-259+dgn-489+pulse-140}}. Mastercard is the most visible. Growing the relationship, and leaving it

After go-live, every network routes growth through a named relationship owner: Visa's Client Success manager and account team, Mastercard's account managers, Amex relationship managers and Discover account executives {{rcite:visa-386+visa-370+mastercard-638+amex-377+dgn-148}}. Incentives are the main commercial lever, worth 15.75 billion dollars at Visa and 20.5 billion at Mastercard in their 2025 years {{rcite:visa-267+mastercard-105}}. Tracking happens inside the network. Mastercard contracts carry clawbacks and customer commitments, and DOJ alleges Visa can claw back paid incentives as early termination fees {{rcite:mastercard-636+cross-479+cross-480}}.

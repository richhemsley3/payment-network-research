The United States debit networks compete under one rule. Regulation II bars an issuer or a network from restricting a debit card to fewer than two unaffiliated networks, forbids a network from limiting an issuer's other contracts, and forbids inhibiting a merchant's routing choice, with card-not-present transactions covered since July 1, 2023 {{cite:cross-001+cross-002+cross-003+cross-005}}. The interchange cap for issuers above 10 billion dollars in assets remains 21 cents plus five basis points, with a one cent fraud adjustment, and the 2023 proposal to lower it has no final rule as of September 2026 {{cite:cross-007+cross-008+cross-013}}. The Federal Reserve's 2024 data give the networks their only comparable numbers: 37 cents average interchange on dual-message networks against 25 on single-message, and among the single-message networks PULSE and NYCE at 27 cents, Accel at 25, STAR, SHAZAM, AFFN and CULIANCE at 23 and Jeanie at 17 {{cite:cross-024+cross-026}}. The Board warns that these averages blend pricing programmes and do not say what an issuer earns on a given network {{cite:cross-027}}. Everything else a partner would want, the switch fee, the membership fee, the rulebook, is unpublished at every one of them.

1. The front doors split into platforms and gateways. Visa, Mastercard, American Express, AP+, Elo and UnionPay put the catalogue, the documentation and the sign-up on the public side and keep only credentials behind the account. Discover's Developer Center, JCB Partner Online, EASI and the PULSE client website ask the reader to identify themselves before showing anything {{cite:visa-w-dev}} {{cite:mastercard-w-dev}} {{cite:dgn-w-dev}}. Discover is the only one of the four global card networks whose developer centre shows no product list and no documentation to a stranger {{cite:dgn-001+dgn-002}}.
  2. An account buys a sandbox, and a contract buys production, everywhere. Visa issues production credentials only after a signed agreement, Mastercard mints them inactive until it approves, American Express reviews each access request by hand {{cite:visa-028+mastercard-005+amex-386}}. No sandbox in this study replays production data, and test cards are public only on the acquiring side {{cite:visa-191+mastercard-013+visa-227}}.
  3. Interchange is often public, and what the network itself charges almost never is. Visa, Mastercard, Interac, eftpos and Cartes Bancaires publish interchange schedules {{cite:visa-238+mastercard-091+interac-013+eftpos-014+cb-015}}. Only Interac publishes its switch fee to the tenth of a cent, and the card networks publish their own assessments for Canada alone {{cite:interac-011+visa-251+amex-155+dgn-066}}. Regulators are forcing the rest into the open: UK fee transparency from July 2027 and Australian scheme fee roadmaps by April 2027 {{cite:cross-064+cross-074}}.
  4. Visa, Mastercard, Elo and girocard publish their rules, and no US debit network does. The Visa rulebook runs to 923 pages, Mastercard's to four books, Elo's to 694 pages, and girocard publishes its admission contract {{cite:visa-284+mastercard-171+elo-008+girocard-023}}. The American Express network manual, Discover's operating regulations and every PIN network's rules are confidential {{cite:amex-036+dgn-106+pulse-017+shazam-016}}. Mastercard announces rule changes inside Mastercard Connect, behind its sign-in {{cite:mastercard-180+mastercard-w-connect}}.
  5. The Discover family asks a partner to sign in in more places than any other network. The Fraud and Risk Center, EASI, the Advanced Payments portal, eCentral, the Disputes Portal, the Release Compliance Tool, Web Merchant Boarding, the Developer Center, the Diners Club issuer site and the PULSE client website each have their own login {{cite:dgn-041+dgn-024+pulse-038}}. Visa is consolidating toward one client website that claims more than 150 services, and AP+ has already folded three developer portals into one {{cite:visa-164+eftpos-012}}.
  6. Mastercard is the only network that shows partners the health of every API. Its public status page reports each service for sandbox and production {{cite:mastercard-040}}. Visa runs a status page for its acquiring platform and monthly release notes for its developer platform {{cite:visa-188}}, American Express's changelog stopped in May 2024, and no Discover or PULSE property has either a status page or a changelog {{cite:visa-146+amex-021+dgn-018+pulse-018}}.
  7. Ownership is moving under a third of the field. Capital One now owns Discover, PULSE and Diners Club and has moved its own debit cards onto them, more than doubling network volume in a year {{cite:dgn-087+dgn-056}}. Banks have explored buying STAR, and Fiserv has put its networks under review {{cite:star-017+accel-022}}. Jeanie passed to Global Payments in January 2026, and CULIANCE is an FIS network, separate from the CO-OP network Velera still runs {{cite:jeanie-004+culiance-002+culiance-006}}.

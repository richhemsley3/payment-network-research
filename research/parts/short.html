1. The front doors split into platforms and gateways. Visa, Mastercard, American Express, AP+, Elo and UnionPay put the catalogue and the sign-up on the public side, and most put the documentation there too {{cite:visa-205+mastercard-032+amex-002+eftpos-w-dev+elo-w-dev+unionpay-037}}. Visa still holds SDKs and sample code behind sign-in, American Express locks some API references, and AP+ gates its documentation pages {{cite:visa-207+amex-001+eftpos-012}}. Discover's Developer Center, JCB Partner Online, EASI and the PULSE client website ask the reader to identify themselves before showing any technical documentation {{cite:visa-w-dev}} {{cite:mastercard-w-dev}} {{cite:dgn-w-dev}}. Discover is the only one of the four global card networks whose developer centre opens no documentation to a stranger {{cite:dgn-001+dgn-203}}. Its Explore page still names seven APIs, and its Partner Product Portal publishes OpenAPI specifications for twelve products {{cite:dgn-169+dgn-008+dgn-007}}.
  2. An account buys most sandboxes at Visa and Mastercard, and production waits on a contract or an approval. Visa issues certification and production credentials only after a signed agreement, and Mastercard mints production credentials inactive until it approves {{cite:visa-190+mastercard-003+visa-028+mastercard-005}}. American Express reviews each access request by hand, sandbox included, and Discover admits developers only through a representative {{cite:amex-386+amex-007+dgn-002}}. Visa's acceptance consoles come with default sandbox credentials {{cite:visa-435+visa-436}}. No sandbox in this study replays production data, and test cards are public only on the acquiring side {{cite:visa-191+mastercard-013+visa-227}}.
  3. Interchange is often public, and what the network itself charges almost never is. Visa, Mastercard, Interac, eftpos and Cartes Bancaires publish interchange schedules {{cite:visa-238+mastercard-091+interac-013+eftpos-014+cb-015}}. Only Interac publishes its switch fee to the tenth of a cent, and the card networks publish their own assessments for Canada alone {{cite:interac-011+visa-251+amex-155+dgn-066}}. Canada's Code of Conduct requires that publication {{cite:cross-163}}. Australia will require eftpos, Mastercard and Visa to publish quarterly aggregate scheme fee data from October 2026, and expects scheme fee roadmaps by April 2027 {{cite:cross-078+cross-074}} {{cite:cross-226}} {{cite:cross-227}}. The UK will require Visa and Mastercard to give acquirers clearer information on UK scheme and processing fees from July 2027 {{cite:cross-063+cross-064+cross-065}}.
  4. Visa, Mastercard, Elo and girocard publish their rules, and no US debit network does. The Visa rulebook runs to 923 pages, Mastercard's to four books, Elo's to 694 pages, and girocard publishes its admission contract {{cite:visa-284+mastercard-171+elo-008+girocard-023}}. The American Express network manual, Discover's operating regulations and every PIN network's rules are confidential {{cite:amex-036+dgn-106+pulse-017+shazam-016}}. Mastercard announces rule changes inside Mastercard Connect, behind its sign-in {{cite:mastercard-180+mastercard-w-connect}}.
  5. The Discover family runs ten partner login pages, and no one account opens them. The Fraud and Risk Center, EASI, the Advanced Payments portal, eCentral, the Disputes Portal, the Release Compliance Tool, Web Merchant Boarding, the Developer Center, the Diners Club issuer site and the PULSE client website each have their own login {{cite:dgn-041+dgn-024+pulse-038}}. One Visa Partner account already opens Visa Developer and, after extra validation, Visa Online, which claims more than 150 services {{cite:visa-031+visa-164}}. AP+ has folded three developer portals into one {{cite:eftpos-012}}.
  6. Mastercard and Visa's acquiring platform are the only ones that show API health in public. Its public status page reports each service for sandbox and production {{cite:mastercard-040}}. Visa runs a status page for its acquiring platform, sandbox included, and monthly release notes for its developer platform {{cite:visa-188+visa-439+visa-146}}. American Express's developer portal changelog stopped in May 2024, though its Payment Gateway keeps a versioned changelog {{cite:amex-021+amex-023}}. No public Discover or PULSE site carries a status page or a changelog {{cite:dgn-018+pulse-018}}.
  7. Ownership is moving under a third of the field. Capital One now owns Discover, PULSE and Diners Club and has moved its own debit cards onto them, more than doubling network volume in a year {{cite:dgn-087+dgn-056}}. Banks have explored buying STAR, and Fiserv has put its networks under review {{cite:star-017+accel-022}}. Jeanie passed to Global Payments in January 2026, and CULIANCE is an FIS network, separate from the CO-OP network Velera still runs {{cite:jeanie-004+culiance-002+culiance-006}}.

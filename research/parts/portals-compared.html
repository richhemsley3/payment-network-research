Two shapes of front door

The properties sort into platforms and gateways. A platform puts the catalogue, the documentation and the sign-up on the public side and keeps only credentials behind the account: Visa, Mastercard, American Express, AP+ Developers, Dev.Elo and the UPI Open Platform work this way. A gateway shows a welcome line and asks the reader to identify themselves before anything else: the Discover Developer Center, JCB Partner Online, EASI, the PULSE client website. The Interac Innovation Hub is a platform by shape and a gateway by rule, since access to specific products needs an existing relationship with an issuer {{cite:interac-w-hub}}. Neither shape is wrong for a network that onboards through a sponsor and a contract. The difference is what a stranger can judge before asking to be let in.

What the account buys, and what the contract buys

Every platform draws the same line. The account buys a project, sandbox keys and mock data. The contract buys certification and production. Visa issues certification and production credentials only once a signed agreement is in place {{cite:visa-028}}, and says onboarding can take two weeks or several months {{cite:visa-029}}. Mastercard mints production credentials instantly and leaves them inactive until it approves the production request {{cite:mastercard-005}}, with a third environment, the Mastercard Test Facility, reached by emailing an implementation manager {{cite:mastercard-015}}. American Express grants environment access after a manual review of an access request, with the outcome sent by email {{cite:amex-386}}. Discover asks for the contact form first {{cite:dgn-w-dev}}.

Mock data, and who gets the test cards

No sandbox in this study replays production data. Visa's sandbox is mock data per project {{cite:visa-191}}, Mastercard's returns generated account ranges that are not real BIN ranges {{cite:mastercard-013}}, and American Express describes its sandbox as mock data and static responses {{cite:amex-006}}. Test cards are public on the acquiring side and private on the network side: Visa Acceptance publishes test cards {{cite:visa-184}}, Mastercard Send publishes test PANs that include other networks' {{cite:mastercard-017}}, while Mastercard as a whole has no portal-wide test cards {{cite:mastercard-016}} and American Express emails test cards after integration-setup forms {{cite:amex-009}}. Discover's test card kits sit inside EASI {{cite:dgn-w-site}}.

Change and health

Only Mastercard publishes the health of every API for sandbox and production on a public page, refreshed about once a minute {{cite:mastercard-040}}. Visa Acceptance runs a Statuspage with subscriptions {{cite:visa-188}}, and Visa Developer publishes monthly release notes tagged public or restricted {{cite:visa-146}}. American Express's portal-wide changelog stopped in May 2024 while individual pages carry 2026 dates {{cite:amex-021}}, and its urgent bulletins for network partners are posted inside the gated Knowledge Base {{cite:amex-371}}. No Discover property has a status page or a changelog {{cite:dgn-w-partner}}.

How many doors a partner meets

The number of places to sign in is a fact a partner feels every week. Visa has Visa Developer, Visa Partner, Visa Acceptance and the client website now branded Visa Access, which claims more than 150 services behind one password {{cite:visa-164}}. Mastercard has Developers and Connect. American Express has Developers, the Knowledge Base for network partners, the AMEX Enabled dashboard for service providers and the Online Merchant Account {{cite:amex-362+amex-374+amex-314}}. The Discover family has the Fraud and Risk Center, EASI, the Advanced Payments Providers portal, eCentral, the Disputes Portal, the Release Compliance Tool, Web Merchant Boarding, the Developer Center, a ServiceNow login for Diners Club issuers and the PULSE client website {{cite:dgn-w-site}} {{cite:dgn-w-partner}} {{cite:pulse-w-site}}. The site's own Log In lands on the fraud tools and lists four of the others in a side panel.

Support routes

Support is where the networks differ most in tone. American Express takes a ticket from anyone, states its hours, and keeps product mailboxes {{cite:amex-384}}. Mastercard needs an account for a ticket and offers the forum, the guides and an MCP server to everyone else {{cite:mastercard-w-dev}}. PULSE publishes a phone number, Central-time hours and named mailboxes for disputes, fraud and the processor portal {{cite:pulse-w-site}}. Visa Developer offers FAQs and a contact form, and its community site did not answer on the day {{cite:visa-w-dev}}. Discover's Developer Center offers the contact form and nothing else {{cite:dgn-w-dev}}.

The AI layer, in September 2026

Three of the platforms now put an assistant on the public side. Visa Developer carries an Ask AI Assistant control in its header, its terms define an AI chatbot and an MCP code-generation service, and since 2026 it runs a documentation MCP server and publishes a command-line package for Claude Code {{cite:visa-w-dev}} {{cite:visa-356+visa-224+visa-225}}. Mastercard Developers ships an MCP server that works with Claude, Cursor and other clients, an agent toolkit page in its documentation, and llms.txt files in every footer {{cite:mastercard-w-dev}}. The UPI Open Platform led its hero with agentic payment solutions and MCP query services on the day {{cite:unionpay-w-open}}. American Express sells Agentic Commerce Experiences as a product but offers no assistant on the portal {{cite:amex-w-dev}}.

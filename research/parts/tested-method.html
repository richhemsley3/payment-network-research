The insights went through two rounds. In the first, three challengers attacked each of the original twelve: one audited the evidence, one asked whether it changes a partner's choice, and one argued the opposite case. A judge settled each group, a fresh red team attacked the revisions, and a critic read the set as a whole. Nine survived, three by merging.

The critic found the set leaned on process and said little about why a partner would choose the network. A separate search covered eight areas of partner value: issuer economics, acquirer and merchant economics, reach, ownership, the provider ecosystem, value-added services, the relationship's burden, and market openings. It proposed 54 candidates, and verifiers confirmed 51 with changes. Synthesis and a completeness critic brought them to sixteen.

The second round took all 25 together. For each batch of related insights, one auditor checked every sentence against its source, one named the experience decision it informs, and one placed Discover against the other networks. Judges wrote the cards, a decision map was built from them, and a red team made 18 fixes to the map and 24 to the cards. Every new source entered the competitive report's ledger and was checked for its quote automatically, with agents reading the pages a script could not. The same work corrected the competitive report wherever its prose made a claim the tests showed to be wrong.

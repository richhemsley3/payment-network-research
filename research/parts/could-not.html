1. What any network charges its partners by contract. Issuer rates, network assessments outside Canada, agent registration fees since 2016, production API prices and every US PIN network's switch fees are negotiated and unpublished {{cite:amex-168+visa-030+mastercard-064+dgn-071+pulse-011+star-013}}.
  2. The confidential rulebooks. The American Express network manual, Discover's operating regulations and U.S. acquirer fee chart, PULSE's and SHAZAM's rules and UnionPay's bylaws could not be read {{cite:amex-036+dgn-063+pulse-017+shazam-016+unionpay-031}}. Mastercard's rule announcements sit inside Mastercard Connect, behind its sign-in {{cite:mastercard-180+mastercard-w-connect}}.
  3. The inside of the partner portals. Every gated client portal in this study is described from its login page, its public documentation and public video only. That covers Visa Access, Mastercard Connect, the American Express Knowledge Base and AMEX Enabled dashboard and every Discover client portal. It also covers the PULSE client website, SHAZAM Access, JCB Partner Online, the AP+ member portal, UnionPay's institution portal and girocard's product-development site.
  4. Certification timelines. Visa puts going live on its developer platform at two weeks to multiple months, and Tap to Phone certification at six to twelve months {{cite:visa-029+visa-024}}. Mastercard's Mastercom and Send products publish durations, and Elo charges once custom certification passes 90 business days {{cite:mastercard-078+mastercard-081+elo-022}}. No other certification path in the study states how long it takes.
  5. API rate limits. No network publishes numeric limits. Mastercard's documentation uses placeholders, and Visa and American Express raise limits on request {{cite:mastercard-028+visa-204+amex-024}}.
  6. Volumes and participant counts for the US PIN networks. STAR and Accel publish a combined figure only, and NYCE, SHAZAM, CULIANCE, Jeanie and AFFN publish none. The Federal Reserve's averages are the only comparable numbers {{cite:star-003+cross-026}}.
  7. Sites that refused this study. NPCI and RuPay blocked every fetch, so the RuPay profile rests on secondary sources. The live Cartes Bancaires site and Mastercard's marketing pages were read from archived copies {{cite:rupay-013+cb-028}}. The investor sites of Visa, Mastercard, Capital One and Fiserv returned bot walls, and filings were read on the SEC's site instead.
  8. Most demonstration captions. YouTube stopped answering caption requests partway through the study. Captions were read for 34 videos, and Visa, Diners Club and PULSE demonstrations are described from their listings. No video frames were captured.
  9. Most partner opinion. 219 of 231 network and audience cells hold two public items or fewer. Reddit, LinkedIn and several vendor forums could not be read without an account.
  10. Trade-press corroboration for the later networks. The research agents for the Discover family and the smaller networks ran out of search quota, so their profiles lean on primary documents and carry fewer Reported sources.
  11. claims that failed re-verification. They were dropped from the report and remain in the ledger with the reason. Among them are Mastercard's Canadian and Australian interchange schedules of 2026, which no method could re-read, and a Canadian cross-border assessment whose only readable archive shows a different rate.
  12. What the walk counts measure. Clicks to documentation and clicks to the first wall come from a scripted walk that follows the first matching link. They are comparable between properties and are not a usability measure.

1. Would a published US acquirer schedule win acquirer volume, or only invite rate demands? No source shows publication would win volume, and Capital One's deal case assumed existing network pricing {{rcite:dgn-221}}. Discover's US rates, incentives and acquirer margins are not public {{rcite:dgn-064+dgn-071}}. Interview acquirers on how they present Discover to merchants today. Put the pricing question to the network's business team with those findings.
  2. Do third-party exempt issuers receive the Discover Debit rates advisers quote for Capital One's migrated cards? The rates found are adviser quotes, and whether other issuers receive them is not public {{rcite:dgn-222+dgn-212}}. The Fed's 2024 Discover average mostly reflects Discover's own cards {{rcite:cross-109}}. Ask the network's pricing team. Interview community banks, credit unions and programme managers on Discover Debit yield.
  3. Which originators push to Discover and PULSE cards today, and which do not? The study found no public originator count {{rcite:dgn-304}}. Current eligibility at Branch, PayActiv and PayPal was not checked. Pull originator enablement data from the business. Test payouts to a Discover debit card at the providers Capital One names.
  4. Which announced alliances are live, in which direction, and what volume does each carry? Release dates mark announcements, and live acceptance can follow later. The study found no public alliance volume, and Capital One reports one Global Payment Network figure {{rcite:dgn-344}}. Ask the network team for each alliance's status and volume by direction, and check each against the map's grades.
  5. What do the map's H, G and J tiers measure, and why does 185 sit beside 110 graded markets? The map code labels the tiers and publishes no thresholds {{rcite:dgn-353}}. No page read explains the gap between 185 and 110. Ask the map's owners for tier thresholds and the scope of each count before any grade enters a prototype.
  6. Are the consent order's classification controls in place, and can the network say who sets each card's class? The compliance plans go to the Reserve Bank and are not public {{rcite:cross-113+dgn-245}}. Ask the business and legal before statement copy names who sets each card's class.
  7. Do partner issuers see the owner as a threat to their data or treatment, and would an access log answer it? The study found no public report of a bank leaving PULSE over ownership. The Fed approval also rests on commitments the study could not see in full {{rcite:cross-251}}. Interview community bank and credit union issuers on Discover Debit and PULSE with the access-log prototype. Ask the business what commitments exist.
  8. What is Discover's dispute monitoring line, and does a refund after a Fraud Alert keep a dispute out of the count? Discover's dispute rules are not public, and its line surfaces only through vendors {{rcite:dgn-122+dgn-288}}. The gated Disputes Portal may hold it. Ask the network's disputes team. Interview acquirers on whether they prefer a published line or case-by-case handling.
  9. What do the gated partner portals already offer? The study read public pages only. EASI, the Disputes Portal and the signed-in Partner Product Portal may hold notices, incident messages, merchant reporting and API keys {{rcite:dgn-164+dgn-386+dgn-231+dgn-204}}. Walk the gated portals with the network team before Clearway duplicates anything they hold.
  10. Which network commitments would partners plan around, and would they rely on a network's per-partner reading of a notice? Visa says timing is nearly every client's first question, while Mastercard tells Send partners not to plan on its ranges {{rcite:visa-427+mastercard-081}}. Mastercard defines announcements as part of its Standards {{rcite:mastercard-179}}. No source measures partner reliance. Ask acquirers, issuers and processors in interviews which commitments and readings they would act on. Feed the answers to the insight 5 ruling and to legal.
  11. If Rich's d6 ruling admits network commitments, would one portal-wide support period and one severity-tiered support promise set Discover apart? Both are timings, and the ruling is open (insight 5). None of the four networks states a portal-wide support period {{rcite:visa-437+mastercard-021+amex-022+dgn-018}}. Among Mastercard's product support pages, only Ethoca Alerts sets targets by severity {{rcite:mastercard-402+mastercard-401}}. Rich rules on decision 6, then the business says whether it will stand behind each commitment.
  12. How would a large issuer read a deal view that shows the incumbent incentives it puts at risk? The study has no source on how issuers would read such a view. Individual deal and clawback terms are not public {{rcite:mastercard-105}}. Test the agent deal flow with partner managers, then with a small set of issuer prospects.
  13. What does PULSE pay an ATM deployer per withdrawal, and do the reissued debit cards carry another ATM network? Neither is public. PULSE Select's product page now redirects, so whether it still enrols institutions is unclear {{rcite:pulse-082}}. Ask PULSE for its current ATM schedule and Capital One for the reissued cards' ATM marks. Interview ATM deployers on what they need before joining.
  14. Can a US programme manager get commercial ranges on Discover, and do DCX and the Restricted Access Network work on them? No source found answers either question, and Discover's US interchange schedule is confidential {{rcite:dgn-063+dgn-064}}. Ask the business, then confirm with a processor that carries Discover, such as Marqeta or SoFi Tech Solutions.
  15. Who should bear the loss when a registered agent misreads an instruction on a card issued on Discover Network, and at what cost? Worldpay says no liability shift exists yet, and no source prices the liability {{rcite:cross-220+amex-130}}. EMVCo's framework is a draft open for comment until 30 September 2026 {{rcite:cross-264}}. Put a proposed dispute rule to the business and legal, and revisit it once EMVCo publishes its final framework.

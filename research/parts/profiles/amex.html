American Express Company · New York · network partners in about 110 countries and territories · issuers, acquirers and processors, merchants, service providers, fintechs, developers

Amex sells a licence, then hands the rules over. A partner on the American Express network is a third party licensed as an issuer or an acquirer, and nothing that binds it is public before the licence: the network rulebook, the fee schedule and the specifications all arrive after signature {{cite:amex-035+amex-036+amex-038}}. What a stranger can read is the merchant side, a developer portal of sixteen products, and a service-provider certification programme that charges nothing to join {{cite:amex-241+amex-002+amex-047}}.

Three programmes, three doors

Global Network Services licenses banks to issue Amex-branded cards or to acquire local merchants, and monetises the licence as royalties, fees and per-transaction amounts {{cite:amex-283+amex-286}}. The issuer rate each partner is paid is negotiated and never published {{cite:amex-168}}. Certification is a mandatory network service for both sides: issuers complete host and payment-product certification, acquirers complete host and terminal certification {{cite:amex-040+amex-039}}, with an eight-step representative-led host process, a self-accreditation route for qualifying acquirers and a free host simulator {{cite:amex-042+amex-044+amex-045}}. Fintechs do not licence directly. They arrive through issuer-processors admitted to the Agile Partnership Platform, Marqeta in February 2025 and Lithic in May 2025, with a sponsor bank issuing {{cite:amex-074+amex-097+amex-075}}. The public fintech page carries positioning and no application {{cite:amex-076}}.

OptBlue is the acquiring door. Acquirers, processors and payment facilitators contract directly with small merchants and set the price. Amex charges the merchant no wholesale fee and the participant is the merchant's single servicing contact {{cite:amex-300+amex-392}}. Thirty-two participants are named on the public page {{cite:amex-227}}. What is not public is how an acquirer becomes one. Amex publishes no process for joining OptBlue, and the participant rulebook is named but not published {{cite:amex-061+amex-432}}. Payment facilitators under 250 million dollars of annual Amex volume ride under a certified participant {{cite:amex-271}}. Canada is the one market where the wholesale schedule is public, tiered by industry and ticket size with a 0.12 percent participation fee and a 0.30 percent card-not-present fee {{cite:amex-153+amex-155}}.

AMEX Enabled is the door for everyone who builds: terminal, card, mobile, 3-D Secure and tokenisation providers, laboratories and platforms register in three steps, pay nothing, and work inside a dashboard with a certification journey, a document library and DocuSign contracts {{cite:amex-046+amex-047+amex-342}}. Registration is not approval, since a product must still pass certification before anyone may use it {{cite:amex-049}}. The programme's own entry hostname did not answer on the day of reading and the working entry is the service-provider page {{cite:amex-375}}. POS software vendors join the Integrated Payments Providers track with a named team for test cards and certification questions {{cite:amex-378+amex-379}}.

What a licensed partner sees

Once licensed, the partner lives in the Knowledge Base, the gateway to every network service: FraudNet, OpsNet, File Tracker, the Token Requester tool, service enrolment and a participant directory {{cite:amex-400+amex-362}}. Access requests are handled by GNS User Services with a stated three-business-day turnaround {{cite:amex-401}}. Policy arrives as manuals twice a year, interim changes as the monthly bulletin, and specification mandates six months ahead {{cite:amex-403+amex-249+amex-251}}. Urgent operational notices are posted inside the Knowledge Base, not on a status page {{cite:amex-371}}. Training is a named function, Network Partner Learning Solutions, with an essentials curriculum, six compliance modules covering more than fifty policies, and webinars in two formats {{cite:amex-363+amex-365+amex-366}}. The public webinar list has not been updated since 2022 and the public news hub carried no dated articles when read {{cite:amex-369+amex-412}}.

Twenty-four months

The window opened with two merchant class actions attacking the anti-steering rules under OptBlue {{cite:amex-080+amex-081}} and a 230 million dollar resolution of the small-business sales-practices investigations in January 2025 {{cite:amex-086}}. Leadership swapped in February 2025, with Anna Marrs taking Global Merchant and Network Services {{cite:amex-090}}. The network opened to fintechs through Marqeta and Lithic, and Coinbase launched a card on it {{cite:amex-097+amex-098}}. The OptBlue ceiling tripled in April 2026 {{cite:amex-095}}. The same month Amex unveiled the Agentic Commerce Experiences developer kit with a purchase-protection promise for registered agents, and later agreed to buy Hypercard, a fintech that had launched on the network through the Agile Partnership Platform {{cite:amex-126+amex-127+amex-132}}. In May 2026 Amazon moved its business co-brand to U.S. Bank on Mastercard {{cite:amex-136}}. In August 2026 Amex signed three Thai banks as acquiring partners {{cite:amex-149}}. The reintroduced Credit Card Competition Act exempts cards where issuer and network are commonly owned, which is Amex's position {{cite:amex-113+amex-114}}.

Elo Serviços · Brazil · owned by Banco do Brasil, Bradesco and Caixa · issuers, acquirers, sub-acquirers, wallets, processors, developers

Elo publishes a 694-page rulebook and lets anyone apply. The Regulamento dos Arranjos de Pagamento ELO, version 13.0 of May 11, 2026, defines every participant modality from post-paid issuer to sub-acquirer, gives participants 21 days to comment on changes, and states that any entity meeting its requirements may apply in writing and may appeal a refusal {{cite:elo-008+elo-020+elo-010+elo-019}}. Interchange is capped in the rulebook at 0.5 percent on debit and 0.7 on prepaid domestic purchases, while the fee annex describes every other mechanism and leaves the amounts to the partner portal {{cite:elo-014+elo-015}}.

Owner and reachA Brazilian company since 2011. More than 132 million cards in secondary figures, international acceptance through Discover and Diners Club since 2016.{{cite:elo-001+elo-002+elo-003|Elo, Reported}}
JoiningWritten application, admission or justified refusal, a joining fee sized on certification time, and a monthly portal fee per company. Custom certification beyond 90 business days is charged.{{cite:elo-019+elo-017+elo-022|Elo rulebook}}
FeesA brand licence fee as a percentage of issuer turnover, an acquirer brand fee by merchant category and volume, and international fees in dollars.{{cite:elo-016+elo-018|Elo rulebook}}
ServicesIts own token service provider, the Áquila fraud score, ATM products, stand-in, benchmarking reports and a consulting line, Elo Performance e Insights.{{cite:elo-024+elo-023+elo-025+elo-027+elo-028|Elo rulebook, Elo}}
Disputes and complianceFour chargeback stages, each with a possible fee. A mandatory compliance programme and a merchant audit programme in the annex.{{cite:elo-011+elo-012|Elo rulebook}}
SupportThe Centro de Operações Elo for second-level issues and partner tickets, a paid Universidade Elo, and a rulebook forum for releases.{{cite:elo-031+elo-032|Elo rulebook}}

## Rules applied

- **Differentiator** needs all three: no incumbent (Visa, Mastercard, Amex) offers a good version, the evidence shows it matters to partners, and an incumbent could not match it within a release or two because of structure, ownership, assets the Discover family already holds, or scale of change. Anything else is parity (incumbent already does it, or a quality bar an incumbent could reach quickly) or none.
- **Severe** needs evidence that partners repeatedly lose money, time or control, or that regulators or courts stepped in on that task. Ratings resting on allegations (the DOJ complaint against Visa) or on a single vendor were downgraded to notable, and the why text says so.
- **Merges** keep the task in the stage where the partner meets it. Evidence from the absorbed task was carried over. Stage-specific new: ids from the other stage were replaced by their ledger twins in sources.json (identical claim text) or by the destination stage's own key.
- Every id in the output resolves: ledger ids in sources.json, new: keys in the task's own stage file. Every stage_move title matches a design move in its stage file.

## Differentiators kept

- **1-4 Model the yield and weigh incumbent incentives**: A self-serve debit yield model. No network lets a prospect see its yield or price self-serve, and advisers say community institutions sign without benchmarks. Incumbent debit pricing runs on confidential negotiated incentives (confidentiality reported to the RBA, cliff pricing alleged by the DOJ), so a transparent model cuts against their structure, while PULSE already runs the Debit Advantage Analysis with ABA due diligence behind it.
- **3-3 Certify the host with the network**: Live simulator results with a committed review. Discover's designated simulator already gives live results with no logs to submit and review under agreed service levels. Visa schedules attended tests and Mastercard needs licensed simulators and log review. Amex offers a free simulator, yet its process stays representative-led across eight steps. Matching means re-platforming host testing, not a release.
- **3-5 Certify once for many clients**: Build for the processor as the customer. No network lets an intermediary act for all its clients with a read-only view for each. Mastercard has pieces (terminal reuse, an on-behalf flag in its developer portal) and Visa registers agents per acquirer. Matching needs entitlement changes across every incumbent portal. This is the closest call of the eight.
- **6a-5 Review and pay the network invoice**: The one-page bill as the default view. No incumbent offers PULSE's all-in fee on a one-page invoice, and community banks and credit unions complain of long bills. Incumbents bill many fee lines, so matching means repricing, not a release. Mastercard's simplified invoice (mastercard-503) shows the direction but not the one-page bill.
- **6a-6 Trace scheme fees to their triggers and pass them on**: Every fee line traced to its trigger. Visa and Mastercard told the UK regulator they could not supply fee logic within the 12-month window, and acquirers with over 90 percent of the UK market struggle with behavioural fees.
- **6c-1 Triage the week's network bulletins**: A change ledger with a verdict for each partner. No network tells a partner whether its processor has already absorbed a change, and processors write that verdict today. Incumbents send private, high-volume bulletins, so per-partner verdicts are a large-scale change for them, while Discover's light change load makes it tractable.
- **6c-2 Implement a twice-yearly release or mandate**: A quiet cycle, said by the network. Discover's light change load is structural: Marqeta's 2026 log files 41 Visa items against 1 Discover item, and processors say Discover mandates asked nothing of clients. Incumbents cannot quieten their twice-yearly releases quickly, and partners report compressed timelines.
- **7-2 Track incentive commitments and payments**: Incentive ledger in the partner record. No partner-facing incentive tracker was found at any network, and Visa and Mastercard estimate earned incentives from forecasts and client data. Opacity is built into incumbents' bespoke incentive contracts, while Discover's small incentive book makes transparency cheap.

Nearest misses, kept as parity: 6c-5 core network status (strong evidence, but a status page is release-sized), 5-7 conversion kit (Visa Account Updater already carries conversions into Visa), 7-1 business review (Visa Analytics Platform already offers self-service peer benchmarks), 6b-2 free pre-dispute resolution (Visa switched RDR on for every issuer, and dropping a fee is a pricing choice).
